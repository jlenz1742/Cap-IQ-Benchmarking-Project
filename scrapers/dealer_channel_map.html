Sales channel strategy

A flat “1,819 dealers” count hides more than it tells you. The network is bimodal: one corporate paint chain supplies four doors in ten, a handful of true product specialists supply well under one in ten, and roughly 620 independently owned shops — most stocking Festool at a single counter — supply four more in ten. Treating all of them as one channel means either over-servicing the long tail or under-investing in the tier that actually sells the system.

The network runs at two speeds

Festool's own site badges dealers as “Full Line,” “Paint and Drywall,” or “Limited Selection.” The cluster data explains why: Sherwin‑Williams' 709 corporate-owned stores are almost certainly the reach layer — sanders, dust extractors, and abrasives sold alongside a paint or drywall job, not a considered system purchase. The 123 doors at Woodcraft, Rockler, and Acme Tools are the opposite: staffed by woodworkers, built around demo events, and the only channel equipped to sell the Domino, the track saw, and the full dust-extraction line as a system. Both matter; neither should be run on the other's playbook.

Where the map has gaps

Geographically, the top five states — California, New York, Massachusetts, Pennsylvania, and Illinois — hold 675 doors, 37.1% of the network, tracking dense, high-income renovation markets. Texas, with a housing-starts volume that rivals any state in the country, carries just 61 doors; Oklahoma, Kansas, Nebraska, and Arkansas each carry fewer than ten. That gap reads less like low demand and more like an under-recruited Sun Belt channel.

By category, the mismatch is sharper: 56.7% of doors are paint stores against roughly 17% that are true woodworking or tool specialists. A brand built around professional woodworkers and finish carpenters is, by door count, mostly retailed through paint counters.

Three moves, in order

  1. Split the KPI by tier instead of reporting one blended dealer count — doors served for the Anchor tier, revenue per door for Specialty Flagship, stocking depth for Regional Captains. A single number currently hides which tier is actually underperforming.
  2. Fund the Flagship tier disproportionately to its 6.8% share. It is the only channel selling the full system, and it is cheap to fund at 123 doors — co-op demo dollars, expanded SKU assortment, and staff spiffs go a long way at this scale.
  3. Run a state-level whitespace pass — Texas, Oklahoma, Kansas, Nebraska first — to promote the strongest long-tail independents into named Regional Captains with a standard starter kit and stocking minimum, rather than recruiting cold or leaving the gap unaddressed.

A caveat on the data

This clustering is name-based — a proxy for ownership, not the thing itself. It is strong at the top (the account-ID cross-check above confirms Sherwin‑Williams and Rockler as single chains) and progressively noisier down the tail, where two independents could share an owner without sharing a name, or vice versa. Treat the top 4 tiers as reliable and the long tail's size as directional before this becomes a resourcing decision.
